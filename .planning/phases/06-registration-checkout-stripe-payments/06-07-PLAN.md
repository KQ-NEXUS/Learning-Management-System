<objective>
Turn the tracer's bare order summary into the real review-and-consent step: three separately-recorded
policy acceptances, a server-enforced verification gate, a visible hold countdown whose authority lives
on the server, and the two error states a real checkout produces.

Purpose: REG-04 is the requirement where "it looks right in the UI" and "it is actually true" diverge
most easily. Three checkboxes that all set one flag look identical on screen to three checkboxes that
write three versioned rows, and only one of them can answer "what exactly did this learner agree to, and
to which version, at what time" a year later. Phase 3 deliberately left the refund/cancellation policy
for this phase, so this is the first and only place that record gets created.

The enforcement point matters as much as the record. A disabled Pay button is a courtesy to an honest
learner; it stops nobody who posts directly to the action. This codebase already settled that argument
in Phase 1 — RBAC-06's whole premise is that a direct request bypassing the UI must be refused
server-side — and a consent gate is exactly the same shape of claim.

## Planner finding — D-13's verification gate is stronger than D-13 assumed

D-13 describes a learner who can "register, sign in, and reach the order-summary page while
`PENDING_VERIFICATION`", blocked only at the Pay action. Read against the actual auth implementation,
that state is unreachable: `signIn` refuses any user whose `status !== "ACTIVE"`
(`auth-service.ts:51`), `getActorBySessionToken` returns null for a non-ACTIVE user
(`session-service.ts:35`), and `verifyEmail` sets `status: "ACTIVE"` and `emailVerified` in the same
write (`verification-service.ts:151`). An unverified learner therefore cannot hold a session at all, so
they cannot reach the summary page in the first place.

D-13's *intent* — no payment from an unproven identity — is satisfied more strongly than D-13's
mechanism describes. This plan implements the guard and the banner anyway, as defence in depth against a
future change to the sign-in rule, and states plainly that both are expected to be unreachable through
the normal flow today. Do not "fix" this by relaxing the sign-in status check to make the banner
reachable; that would be a real security regression traded for a demo of a warning message.
Output: the consent record, the two gates, the countdown, and the two error states.
</objective>

`PolicyAcceptance` columns (no migration in this phase): `userId`, `policyType` (plain String),

order at one specific moment.
