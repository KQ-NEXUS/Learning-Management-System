## converge

THE TWO SOURCES AGREE, WHICH IS THE INTERESTING PART

Your reference sets --accent: #12a594 — teal.
Row 179 prescribes #0D9488 — teal.

Those were arrived at independently: one by whoever made your mockup, one from 192 product profiles. Both put teal in an LMS.

So mockup 1's teal recommendation was not a quirk of the database, and this direction takes it — but as the ACCENT beside your blue, not as a replacement for it. Blue stays the brand; teal now means progress, pass and grade; amber means course state.

That is row 179's actual note — "Education teal + course amber + grade green" — with the brand left alone.

## reference

WHAT I TOOK FROM YOUR REFERENCE

· Brand #2457d6 — a touch brighter than your #1c4ed8, and it reads better beside teal.
· 16px card radius. Softer than shadcn's 14, noticeably softer than what you have.
· ONE BIG SHADOW: 0 18px 48px rgba(16,24,40,.10). This is the largest departure from every earlier mockup — shadcn casts 1-3px, your reference casts 48px. Depth is doing the work that borders did before.
· A blue glow under primary buttons, and a 140deg gradient on the brand mark.
· Tight display tracking, -.035em on headings and -.045em on h1.
· Paper #f5f7fb rather than white — cards float on it.

## type

TYPE — WHERE I HAD TO SUBSTITUTE

Your reference uses Aptos and Segoe UI Variable. Neither is on Google Fonts, which is the only font host an Artifact can load, so they would silently fall back and you would be reviewing the wrong face.

Plus Jakarta Sans instead — typography.csv row 13, "Friendly SaaS", best-for reads "SaaS products, web apps, dashboards, B2B". Its own note calls it a modern alternative to Inter, and it carries tight tracking the way Aptos does.

If you are shipping to real browsers, Segoe UI Variable is free on Windows and worth keeping in the stack ahead of it.

## honest

ONE THING TO WATCH

A 48px shadow on every card is a strong look, and it is the thing most likely to date. It reads as current now; hairlines and flat surfaces read as current too, and have for longer.

Compare this against mockup 1 directly — that one removed every shadow because row 179's Style_Priority says "Flat Design". Your reference does the opposite. The database and your reference genuinely disagree here, and I followed your reference because you chose it.

Worth checking the staff tables especially: shadows stack visually when cards sit close together, and the staff screens have the densest layouts in the app.
